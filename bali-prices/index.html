01

Money, before anything else

The rule of thumb: Rp 1,000 is about ₱3.50, so ₱100 buys roughly Rp 28,500. Rupiah notes carry a lot of zeros and Rp 50,000 looks like Rp 5,000 in dim light, so count twice at night markets.

Three habits that save real money

Change money at an authorised changer in Bali, not at your departure airport. Carry cash — warungs, ticket booths, beach gates and parking attendants rarely take cards. And book activities online rather than at the counter, which is covered in section 06.

Dress code

Knees and shoulders covered at every temple. A sarong is lent at the gate and is normally included in the ticket, so there is no need to buy one.

Wet-season tip

West-coast sand at Kuta, Seminyak and Canggu collects washed-up debris and murky water from about November to March. The east and south-east — Nusa Dua, Sanur — stay clear, which is why they are the rainy-season pick.

Book the boat direct

Packages arranged through a hotel typically run two to three times the direct price. Buy from the operator, and compare two or three: quoted 2026 fares run from Rp 100,000 to Rp 195,000 one way for the same crossing.

Rainy season, read this

Rough seas cancel crossings with little notice between November and March. Never put Penida on your last day — leave a spare day to try again — and expect the Kelingking descent to be harder on wet steps.

A banana boat is ₱0 cheaper booked ahead

Rp 80,000 online against Rp 300,000 at the counter is about 73% off the identical fifteen minutes. Message an operator on WhatsApp and get the rate confirmed in writing before you turn up.

Buy rides singly, not in combos. A banana boat plus parasailing comes to Rp 330,000 (). The three-ride packages start at Rp 435,000–495,000 and bundle in a jet ski you may not want. Also confirm the weather policy: good operators reschedule or refund parasailing when conditions are unsafe.

Warungs are cash only. Resort enclaves like Nusa Dua have few of them — walk out to the neighbouring village, or eat on your Ubud and Bukit days. Drink bottled or filtered water, and skip ice away from the main strips.

Arrange a driver on WhatsApp before you arrive rather than at a hotel desk, send photos of your luggage so the vehicle is big enough, and get the quote in writing. Some villages are closed to ride-hailing pickups, so a chartered car avoids being stranded.

09

The tourist levy

Per person
Rp 150,000
· once per entry
Who pays
Everyone
Including children and infants

Every foreign visitor pays a one-time levy of Rp 150,000 on entry to Bali, separate from any visa requirement. Pay it on the official Love Bali portal before you fly and keep the QR voucher on your phone. Visitors who have not paid can be refused entry.

Levy scams are still running

Pay only through the official portal. Never scan a QR code handed to you by someone at the airport or at a checkpoint, and keep your own voucher as proof. Children on a foreign passport pay the full amount — there is no age discount.

Visa rules depend on your passport, so check what yours needs before booking. The levy is charged on top of whatever that is.
