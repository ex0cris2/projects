Why Nusa Dua is the right base in December

Most Bali beaches get murky water and washed-up debris in the wet season. Nusa Dua keeps clear water and is the island's go-to rainy-season beach. Everything on the Bukit peninsula is inside 40 minutes; Ubud and Canggu are the only long hauls.

Free, and walkable from the hotel

Water Blow on Peninsula Island — waves funnel through a cliff gap and shoot up to 30 m. Free, parking only, best at mid-to-high tide.
Nusa Dua Promenade — paved 7 km coastal walkway.
Pantai Mengiat and Geger — public beaches despite the resorts.
Puja Mandala — five houses of worship side by side, 10 minutes away.

How bad is Bali traffic, really

Bad enough to wreck a plan. Nusa Dua to Ubud is 45 km and takes 1.5–2 hours, up to 2.5 in traffic. Canggu is 38 km and can take 2.5 hours. Uluwatu's narrow roads clog before sunset — a 20-minute hop can stretch to 40 or 50. Leave early, and never schedule two far-apart places in one day.

Tourist levy Rp 150,000 each

Every foreign visitor pays Rp 150,000 () once per entry, separate from any visa. Pay on the official Love Bali portal before flying and keep the QR voucher. Children and infants pay too. Visitors who have not paid can be refused entry.

Three levy scams were still running in 2026 — only ever pay on the official portal, never a QR code handed to you at the airport.

Places

Markets, food, accessories and souvenirs. Drive times are from the hotel. Tap a heading to open it.

Markets & trendy shops free to browse

Sukawati Art Market — Jl. Raya Sukawati, Gianyar. The one locals and resellers use: it's a distribution hub for handicrafts across the island, so prices beat the tourist markets. Go 8–11am, cooler and calmer. ~1h15 on an Ubud day.

Ubud Art Market — opposite Ubud Palace. Silk scarves, baskets, paintings, carvings. Bargaining expected. Pairs with the Ubud day.

Seminyak Flea Market — Kayu Aya Square, just off the beach. The trendy one: crochet tops, rattan and macramé bags, shell jewellery, hats, artisan décor. Prices start higher than rustic markets but the styling is better. 50 min.

Love Anchor, Canggu — Jl. Pantai Batu Bolong 56, weekends only. Samadi Sunday Market, also Canggu, Sundays 8am–4pm, organic produce and artisan goods. Both 1h30+ — only worth it if you're already up that way.

Badung Market, Denpasar — Bali's largest traditional food market, for the atmosphere rather than souvenirs. Kumbasari art market sits across the river. Go early; it gets rough by midday.

Boutiques: Jl. Kayu Aya in Seminyak is the boutique strip — Kim Soo Home for Indonesian textiles and homeware, plus concept stores up in Canggu. Bali Collection in Nusa Dua is the no-drive option.

Resto on site → 25 min

Most Nusa Dua restaurants sit inside the resorts, which makes a casual meal awkward and expensive. The standalones are where the value is.

Standalone, beach or ocean views: Santai Beach House, Le Bleu, Manarai Beach House, Kulkul Beach House. Piasan is the one people name for steak.

Jimbaran seafood on the sand — grills set out on the beach at sunset, 25 minutes away. The best group dinner on this side of the island and easier to seat ten than anywhere in Nusa Dua.

Resort fine dining, if you want one big night: Koral at Kempinski (tables beside an aquarium), Soleil at The Mulia, Bejana at the Ritz-Carlton, Arwana at The Laguna, Cucina at Sofitel, Kayuputi. Expect Rp 200,000–400,000 a head and up — see the Prices tab.

Cheap and good: warungs in Bualu village just outside the resort gates, Rp 20,000–35,000 a plate. Cash only.

Accessories jewellery · piercings · tattoos

Silver is Bali's craft. Celuk village in Gianyar has worked silver for over a century — in the 1970s almost the whole town switched from farming to silversmithing, and it's still the island's centre. Real Balinese silver is 925: 92.5% silver, 7.5% copper.

Where: Prapen Jewellery (Jl. Jaga Raga 66, Celuk, daily 9–5), Sunaka Jewelry (Jl. Jagaraga 28B — founded 1979, also at Beachwalk Kuta and in Ubud), Chez Monique in Ubud, and the John Hardy flagship at Mambal. For recycled and ethical pieces, Luna & Rose and Ananda Soul.

Make your own. Celuk runs hands-on silver classes — you leave with the piece. A good rainy-afternoon option on an Ubud day, and a better souvenir than anything off a stall.

Body jewellery — nose rings, belly rings, anklets, body chains — is widely sold, often handmade and far cheaper than at home.

Piercings and tattoos. There are roughly 280 tattoo studios on the island and quality varies a lot. Seminyak (50 min) has the strongest hygiene and international credentials; Canggu (1h30+) is the densest, with many artists trained in Europe or Australia; Kuta (35 min) has the most walk-ins and the widest spread. Names that recur: Artful Ink, Mason's Ink, Angel Ink, Canggu Ink Club, Amor Fati.

Same rules for a piercing as a tattoo: needles and sterile packs opened in front of you, single-use only, named-brand ink clearly labelled. Anything opened early, or ink in unmarked bottles, means walk out. Both need healing time before swimming — on a beach trip, that's a real conflict, so do it at the end or not at all.

Tourist spots & souvenir goods what to buy

The spots themselves — temples, terraces, waterfalls and beaches, with every entrance fee — are in the Prices tab, and the drive times are on the Map.

Krisna Oleh-Oleh is the efficient souvenir run: Southeast Asia's largest souvenir chain, fixed prices so there's no haggling, plus a food court. Branches on Jl. Raya Tuban in Kuta and Sunset Road. Best single stop if you're buying for everyone back home.

Bali Collection, inside the Nusa Dua complex, needs no drive at all — Padi Art Market inside carries crafts, shell work, herbs and spices from Gianyar craftsmen, alongside Luluca, Alun Alun Indonesia, Sikut Satak and a duty-free store.

Also one-stop: Erlangga 2 in Denpasar and The Keranjang Bali.

What's actually worth carrying home: Balinese coffee, pie susu, batik and textiles, sarongs, wood carving, 925 silver, incense and sandalwood, coconut-oil cosmetics. Leave room — and note the foldable bag on the packing list.

Haggling: expected at markets, never at Krisna or in boutiques. Open around half the asking price and settle near two-thirds. Vendors often believe the first sale of the day brings luck, so early mornings get better prices.

Coffee 5–25 min

In Nusa Dua: Kopi Petani, 25:PM Coffee, Patia Coffee, DUA Kafe (Jl. Pantai Mengiat), Rehat Cafe, Honey and Bread (Pepito building), Secret Cafe. Coffee runs 25,000–50,000, mains from 40,000.

Jimbaran, 25 min: Mövenpick Café (Jl. Wanagiri 1), Bull's Coffee, Teraskota Coffee & Eatery.

When it rains indoor

Close by: a massage or spa afternoon — the default wet-season move, and cheap. GWK's indoor museum floors (9th and 23rd) stay dry. Bali Collection for shopping and a meal.

Worth the drive: Trans Studio Bali, an indoor theme park in Denpasar, tickets from 500,000 (). Beachwalk in Kuta. Icon Bali Mall in Sanur, which has an indoor floating market. Cinema XXI from 40,000 ().

Make something: a cooking class, silver jewellery in Celuk or Ubud, Mason Chocolate's factory workshop, pottery, or Hatten Wines' Cellardoor tasting in Sanur. For a group: escape rooms in Sanur, or axe throwing at Finns Recreation Club in Canggu.
